Dynamite

Busy is not the same as done.

You and two to nine friends run a cartoon dynamite factory. Every station is fun. The most fun station is the one that will ruin everything if you don't stop.

Powder is a slot machine. Casing is a rhythm game. Assembly is a two-person jig. Shipping is a truck with a horn. Nobody is in charge.

By minute three the floor is covered in kegs nobody asked for. By minute five, four of you are crowded around one jig asking each other "what can I do?" At minute nine the truck honks and everyone abandons their station.

The report card tells the Powder player they were 98% — EXCELLENT ★ and the factory it got a D.

And if nobody stops — the pile is powder, the Slammer throws a spark, a fuse hisses for six seconds, and the factory explodes. That's how you lose.

Then somebody says "next round, nobody touch the powder until I say" — and now it's a game.

Where we are

The plan is settled enough to build. This page is the record plus the first visual pass at the floor, how players interact, and what the mini-games look like — with a few picks at the end that save back to me.

1The pitch

Overcooked's chaos, RV There Yet's shared consequences, Factorio's flow — pointed at a joke about software teams that the game never says out loud.

The core joke is the core mechanic

Local productivity ≠ system throughput.

Every player can be flat-out busy — combos climbing, machines thunking — while the factory ships almost nothing. The game never explains this. It arranges for it to happen, then shows you the numbers.

The allegory (WIP limits, bottlenecks, Brooks's Law) is real and never spoken. Players who know it laugh. Players who don't learn it by saying "stop making powder" to a friend.

Theme & tone

Cartoon explosives, zero realism. Boom Powder Blast Beans Fuse Goo Danger Dust Gold Executive Dynamite

Failure is smoke, soot, shaking, confetti and unreasonable paperwork. Nobody is ever hurt. A Hotfix return goes POOF on the dock and leaves a spill to sweep.

ComparableWhat we take

Not an agile training sim, not a management sim, not competitive. No tooltips explaining Brooks's Law. The only leaderboard is the fake one on the report card that praises whoever hurt the factory most.

2Design doctrine

Your steer — "the games that produce a lot or are easy should be the most fun" — is the first law. Everything else follows from it.

Three axes — depth, not difficulty ADR 0009

"Harder" conflates three things. Slower stations get deeper, never harder to execute. Fast stations get a greed ceiling.

AxisRule
Execution difficultyFlat. Two floors, always in ms: no OK under ±100, no Perfect under ±45. Downstream never harder to execute than upstream.
Decision depthRises downstream. QA reads tolerances. Packing plans a drop. Assembly chooses to seat a dented casing. One in-loop decision per station; the rest lives at the meta layer.
Skill ceilingEverywhere. At Tier S it's a greed ceiling, chosen per rep (the Slammer's Heat), never success-driven — a combo-scaled ceiling makes the spark a function of skill and is farmable.

One gesture per station — one idea, not one key; arrows wherever direction is the verb, always the same hands you walk with. A named tell from across the room. A real Perfect everywhere. Combos, shake, confetti, factory-wide stings are Tier S only.

The Groove & the Churn

One shared 0–100 meter. Up when the factory flows, down with WIP, clutter, rework, starvation, lit fuses. Asymmetric: Groove makes the game more rewarding, never easier. Churn makes it more frictional, never impossible.

0 MELTDOWN30 CHURN70 GROOVE85 ON FIRE
  • ≥70: music layers, conveyors glow, score ×1.25, PERFECTs chain across stations. ≥85: ×1.5 and sparkles.
  • ≤30: klaxon, red wash, Casing needle jitters, spills ×2, Standup can fire.
  • Comeback — Clean Sweep: floor at zero + shelves under half for 3 s snaps Groove to 60.

3The factory floor

Top-down, 32 px tiles, server-authoritative movement. WIP is a thing on the floor, not a number. This is the first visual pass at the MVP map — squares with faces are the M1 art budget, on purpose.

Station anatomy

. . . . . . . . . I . W W . R . I input shelf (pull from here) . . [MACHINE] . . W work tiles — the physical staffing cap . O . W W . T . O output shelf R rack T tool cart f f f f f f f f f floor ring — the pile

If all work tiles are occupied you cannot join. You stand in the aisle. Shelves are finite. The floor ring is where the pile goes, and it slows that tile to 40%.

The overflow ladder ADR 0012

Shelftidy, capped; output auto-shelves, no input
Handsshelf full? it's in your hands; the machine waits; the combo ticks
Floorwhere you put it — E at your feet, or Space to throw
Boomthe pile is fuel; one spark and it chains — §5

The game never says stop and the machine never spills for you. The pile is made of kegs someone chose to put down, one at a time, to keep a combo going. Jam isn't a rung any more — it's an oops: an item thrown or knocked onto a machine's work tile jams it (hold the lever 4 s, WAITING FOR VENDOR SUPPORT).

Congestion, made visible

The staffing curve is the authoritative throughput calc — simple and tunable. The physical layer makes the curve look like a cause, not a debuff.

3+ overExtras in the aisle; carriers can't reach the input shelf → starved of parts while surrounded by people. Every ~6 s an extra emits "what can I do?" and the active mini-game pauses 0.5 s.

The Clipboard

Join a station you haven't worked in 60 s → 3 seconds reading a clipboard. Never longer; helping must stay worthwhile. Cross Training upgrade halves it.

▸ Locating the good stapler… ▸ Reading the wiki. The wiki is wrong. ▸ Asking Dave. Dave is at lunch. ▸ Waiting for VPN.

Movement

WASD / arrows, 8-direction, ~4 tiles/s. Soft-collide: players push, not block — an "excuse me!" bump costs 0.3 s. Carrying slows you 20%. Camera: the whole floor, fixed (a pick at the end).

5The Boom

The factory exploding is the only way to lose. Everything else is a bad report card. And it is never random: the pile is fuel you put down, the spark is a careless act you did, and the fuse gives you six seconds.

Do the arithmetic

Powder's floor ring is 9 tiles. Its shelf is 6. A full ring next to a full shelf is 15 kegs. One redlined release and the factory is gone. Going fast and sloppy on the most fun station makes the fuel and the spark. Fast and clean makes only fuel.

Three warnings, always

  1. The pile. It's on the floor.
  2. DANGER beacon at 50% of threshold — the same pole that glows gold at a 25× combo goes red.
  3. The fuse. Six seconds of hiss.

Ignore all three and the explosion is deserved — and blamed on each other, not the game.

Defenses

  • Don't have a pile.
  • Stomp — stand on it, hold Space 1 s.
  • Sweep — spills are fuel.
  • Hot potato — carry it to the Blast Bin. "I got it — oh no."
  • Sand Bucket (M2) — the Overcooked extinguisher.
  • Blast Shield (upgrade) — hidden cost: nobody can see the pile.

Setback mode on Level 1 and Sandbox: stations damaged, day continues. Hard loss from Level 2.

6Stations & what the mini-games look like v2.2

Six screens, mocked at 16:9. The floor stays visible in the top two-thirds; the overlay sits in the bottom third, anchored to the station; the HUD is always on. Every mini-game: ≤ 3 inputs on the hands you walk with, < 10 s, one gesture, a named tell, a real Perfect.

S Boom Powder — The Slammer · mash, then release

The trap, with a greed ceiling. Mash the three chutes ←↑→ (or 1 2 3), each once. Mash speed sets charge speed: 0.3 s → the gauge climbs in 0.7 s with a ±105 ms window, a keg every 1.4 s (8× what Assembly can use). 0.8 s → 1.5 s climb, ±225 ms, every 2.8 s. Every rep is a decision: how hot do I go? DEAD CENTER (±50 ms at any heat) makes a glowing prime keg; late = damp + a spark on the floor ring; early = a grey dud that pours slow at the Jig. A 4th press overflows the hopper into a spill at your feet.

Tell: thunk tempo, hopper glow, the beacon pole. Combo tiers are in-world — 10× a flag, 25× steam + HOT STREAK sting + the pole goes gold, 50× a brass layer and a floor ripple. Kegs read from across the room: grey · steaming · normal · glowing. Shelf full = the keg is in your hands, plunger dark, combo ticking.

S Casing Prep — Stamp & Crimp · tap on the beat, ← → Space

A rhythmic figure, not a metronome: chk · chk · KA-CHUNK. ← → on the two dies (±130 / PERFECT ±60), then both hands on Space for the crimp (±100 / ±45 — the tightest beat in the game, by design). Three PERFECTs = a GLEAM casing that sparkles on the shelf. Stamp miss +1 dent, crimp miss +2. Combo 5 → bar −12%, 10 → −25%; windows fixed in ms, so it tightens as you climb.

Colour is a beat: Q/E arms a switch; the next bar shows a DIE marker in dead time. DUET: two presses on one clock, both crimp PERFECT → KA-CHUNK-CHUNK. The quiet trap: casings are 0 fuel; at 10× two presses flood 53/min of clutter beside Powder's bomb. Churn jitters the needle, not the beat.

A Assembly — The Jig · tap on the beat (Space / ← →) + one toss

The bottleneck. After SEAT (← → picks a casing — dents drawn on the shelf), A's loop is one phrase on the beat at 150 BPM: Space as the fill crosses the mark (5 beats; 7 from a damp keg, 8 from a dud), ←/→ for each fuse knot as it hits the eyelet on its lane (long 3, short 2), Space on the third ratchet click. One timing judge for the whole station. All Perfect on an undented casing = PRIME STICK: one brass flash, one chime, +1 Groove.

B on the rack tile: ← → picks short/long against the next two orders, E spool, E cap, hold Space — an arc sweeps rack → jig — release on the crimp beat. Never walks. A full cradle bounces the toss (B's restraint rule). A missed toss litters the aisle. Tell: zip-zip-zip, alternating hands from across the room; parts arcing every 6 s; silence = starved. Solo ~8.5 s, pair ~6 s.

B QA — The Look · park and stamp

Every order line carries a tolerance — strict / normal / loose — so the same 1-dent stick is a PASS under one card and a FAIL under the next. Condition renders continuously (dings placed randomly, fill line jittering ±4%); the magnifier actually zooms 2×. ←→ park it; E = FAIL tagged with what you parked on; Space = PASS.

Two Perfects: GOOD EYE (a correctly tagged FAIL, which chalks the machine that caused it) and WITHIN SPEC (a PASS on a flawed-but-in-tolerance stick) — so QA isn't silent in a clean factory. Fail a good stick → it comes back tagged WAS FINE. Pass a defect → Hotfix in 120 s; late ones fire during the report and zero the order. Three chalk marks = a red QA strip on that station.

C Packing — Drop Well · drop

A 3-wide well with gravity — an odd width can't be tiled by horizontals, so every crate needs verticals, and a mid-column vertical strands the edge cells. ←→ column, ↑ rotate, ↓ yank, Space drop (thok). Wrong colour bounces off the rim instantly. Q/E picks crate size before the first drop: S 2×2 · M 3×4 · L 3×6.

Lid Bounce: E on the first apex = THOOM (SNUG if every cell is filled); late = RATTLY, narrower OK window at the dock. Cram a 7th stick: strap snaps, never SNUG, Hotfix p 0.5 — right with 8 s on the truck, wrong otherwise. The miss ships: wrong contents slam crooked with a red ? → counts 0 → Returns. Shared shelf + a NEED BLUE ×2 bubble give a helper something real to haul.

C Shipping — The Dock · shove on the beep

The truck's reversing alarm beeps at 1 Hz and the bay light strobes — floor-wide, no overlay needed. Set a crate on your ramp tile, tap Space (or ↑) on a beep: ±70 THUD, springs sag, slot lamp; ±180 scrape; miss = stalls halfway, next beep finishes. Never Returns. Lane = Slot (M2): your ramp tile is your aim.

Clean Load: every crate on consecutive beeps → truck bounce, +2 Groove — only possible if you staged before the horn. Tailgate Slam: last slot fills, hold E 0.5 s → SLAM, horn ×2, truck leaves now — the floor-wide "go back to your stations" cue. In M1 sticks go straight on the truck: Assembly's shelf → dock is a haul, and that hauler is the one who sees the pile.

D The Chore Floor — no combos, no particles, a flat click when it ends

Rework Bench — the Jig in reverse, 6 s tagged / 9 s untagged; finds no defect → the stick goes back to QA WAS FINE. Sweeping — broom, 1.5 s per tile. Unjam — hold E 4 s, WAITING FOR VENDOR SUPPORT.

Hauling — floor keg → shelf; sometimes the right call, still not fun. Stomp — stand on the fuse, hold Space 1 s, ffft. Repair (M2) — hold E 10 s, FILING INCIDENT REPORT.

A well-run factory never touches these. A badly-run one slowly turns into a janitorial simulator. That is the punishment, and it is exactly as fun as it sounds.

7Flow rules

The whole sim is a few hundred lines of pure TypeScript in shared/sim/. The client predicts with it; the server is authoritative with it.

Per station, per tick (20 Hz)

efficiency = curve[clamp(workers)] // 0 → 0 machine = floorSeconds / efficiency * churn * (perfect ? .75 : 1) interrupts = 0.5 * bubblesThisCycle // "what can I do?" cycle = max(execution + interrupts, machine)

Every station is player-paced — the mini-game completes the cycle; a rhythm game with a dead gap is dead. The curve scales the machine's re-arm floor and, above its peak, adds interrupts to the people. Heat and tempo-up let a good player run under nominalSeconds.

Groove, per tick

+8 · onTimeShip −4 · avgShelfFullness −0.5 · floorItems −1 · reworkShelf −2 · starved −1 · jammed −3 · litFuses −2 per fizzle +3 per NICE SAVE

Bottleneck estimate for the report: argmax(utilization × inputShelfFullness).

Utilization is never green. "EXCELLENT" is styled like a corporate certificate — that is the joke. The grade is factory-level only and never fed by the individual column.

Grade

50 · onTime +20 · (1 − peakWIP/target) +15 · quality +15 · cycle vs target −5 per Incident +hero cap 10 +early finish EXPLODED → F

A ≥ 90 ×1.5 · B ≥ 75 ×1.25 · C ≥ 60 ×1.0 · D ≥ 40 ×0.75 · F ×0.5. Finish the book early: +5 per remaining minute. C or better advances; D/F repeats the day — MANAGEMENT WOULD LIKE TO SEE IMPROVEMENT.

P&L — the pile is money on the floor

revenue = shipped × price waste = floor + shelves at close + destroyed + returned coin = max(0, revenue − waste) × grade multiplier

A huge-revenue day with WIP through the roof and three Hotfixes pays less than a modest clean one. That's "relative coin."

10Scaling 3 → 10

The factory grows with the crowd; the stations don't. Ten bodies on one six-station floor is permanent congestion, so above 6 players the one-line template is cloned into two lines (A · B) that share QA, Packing, the Dock and Rework — and the shared downstream is the inter-line bottleneck by design.

PlayersLinesQA lampsBenchesDock lanesTruckOrder book
3–6112460 s11
7–102 (A, B)23645 s20

Fixed at any size: every staffing curve, every mini-game and window, buffer sizes, one vote and one purchase. "Keep it doable": the order book scales sub-linearly because coordination cost is super-linear. Lines are a hat and a HUD label at lobby lock — never a lock.

At 3 congestion never happens; the trap is understaffing — Assembly wants two and someone must stop making powder. Same lesson. Minimum is 3, not 2, because the Jig's pair split needs a second person somewhere.

At 10: ten "what can I do?" bubbles across two lines, a ten-row performance review, and emotes for whoever isn't on the call. "Line B, stop — QA is drowning."

Netcode is a non-issue at 10: Colyseus maxClients: 10, 20 Hz throughout, ~10 KB/s per client typical. M1 is one line (3–6); two lines in M2; a full house of 10 in M3.

11How we build it confirmed

Laravel owns the company. Node owns the factory floor. Reverb is a broadcaster, not a game server — it stays for the lobby and the Payday tallies, not the tick.

The seam: three internal calls

POST /game/ticket"Enter factory". HMAC-signed {userId, factoryId, exp:+60s}; Colyseus onAuth verifies it. Node has no DB creds.
GET /internal/factories/{id}/configRoom create. One JSON blob: level config with owned upgrade patches applied, scaled for the player count.
POST /internal/runsDay end. Report + grade + P&L (computed in shared/sim/grade.ts). Laravel credits coin, opens Payday, broadcasts DayEnded.

Why not Reverb for the sim: it's Pusher-protocol pub/sub — input via HTTP or fire-and-forget client events, no rooms, no state sync. Colyseus is exactly "8 people share a room with binary delta state". Top-down, not iso: the game's key information is who stands where and what's piled where; iso muddies exactly that. The sim stores tile coords, so iso is a renderer swap later.

12Roadmap

M1 is the gate. Nothing past it gets built until "stop making powder" is fun with real humans. Each milestone answers one question.

M0 · Walking Simulator

Does the stack work end to end?

Laravel + auth + Postgres + is_admin. Colyseus room with ticket auth. Phaser scene, tilemap, squares with faces. Debug overlay.

Done: two browsers on two machines see each other walk at 20 Hz.

M1 · The Trap gate

Is "stop making powder" fun?

Slammer, Jig (solo then B's Toss), Dock (bare sticks, beep, tailgate). Items as entities, shelves, hands-full, the pile. Throw + catch + drop table. Fuel, sparks, fuse, chain, explosion (+ setback flag). Hot potato + Blast Bin. Truck ride. Staffing curve + congestion. Clipboard. Shift clock + order book. Report with grade + P&L. Levels 1–4. Sandbox. One line, 3–6.

Done: in 3 playtests with 4+ people who haven't read the docs, someone says "stop making powder" or "no, not everyone" unprompted, and wants to play again.

If not: tune for a week. Still not? Change the core loop before touching M2.

M2 · The Gradient

Is Tier D hated and Tier S loved?

Casing, QA, Packing v2. Rework, Sweep, Unjam, Returns. Groove/Churn with music layers. Hotfix. Sand Bucket, Incidents, sooted sprites. Crate drops, footprints. Two-line tilemap (7–10). Levels 5–6.

Done: playtesters name the station they like and the one they avoid; the avoided ones are all Tier D.

M3 · Chaos

Are interruptions funny the tenth time?

Event system + catalogue. Ambiguous orders + the Phone. Order Board. Full-house playtest at 10. Levels 7–8. Kanban board is a maybe.

Done: no event makes a playtester groan twice.

M4 · The Company

Does persistence bring people back?

Factory screen: coin, corkboard of report cards, level select. Payday: the vote, one purchase, BANK IT, Management's ribbon. Reverb presence + tallies. Admin editor. Levels 9–10. One VPS.

Done: a group plays three days on three evenings and their factory remembers.
